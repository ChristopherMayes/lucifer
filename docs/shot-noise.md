title: "The shot-noise load: the noise power of a beamlet"
short_title: Shot-noise load
---

Self-amplified spontaneous emission (SASE) starts from the [shot noise](glossary.md#gl-shot-noise) of the electron beam: $N$ electrons at independent random phases have a mean square bunching of $1/N$. Before tracking begins, Lucifer generates the [macroparticles](glossary.md#gl-macroparticle) of every [slice](glossary.md#gl-slice). This set of macroparticles is called the [load](glossary.md#gl-load), and the routines that generate it are called the loader.

A slice is represented by far fewer macroparticles than it has electrons. Macroparticles at random phases have a noise of their own, which is determined by their number and not by the beam. The loader therefore proceeds in two steps. It first constructs a load that has no bunching, in which the macroparticles are arranged in [beamlets](glossary.md#gl-beamlet) of evenly spaced phases. This is the [quiet start](glossary.md#gl-quiet-start), `quiet_start = true`, which is the default ([](#shot-quiet)). It then imposes the shot noise of the real beam, `shot_noise = true`, by one of two methods selected by `beam.shot_noise_method`:

- `"weight"`, the default, assigns to every macroparticle a whole number of electrons and leaves the phases unchanged ([](#shot-weight)). The noise power of the load is equal to the shot-noise power at every harmonic below the beamlet size, for any number of electrons, and at the wavelengths between the harmonics. The charge of the bunch is preserved exactly.
- `"first_order"` is the algorithm of Fawley ([](references.md#ref-fawley)), which displaces every phase by a small random amount, the [kick](glossary.md#gl-kick-noise) ([](#shot-kick)). It is the method of Genesis4. Its noise power is less than the shot-noise power when a beamlet represents few electrons: 0.95 of the shot-noise power at 50 electrons per beamlet and 0.16 at one. Away from the harmonics at which the kick is applied, its noise power ranges from 0.004 of the shot-noise power to 4 times the shot-noise power.

The default method is recommended. The method `"first_order"` is intended for comparison with Genesis4, and for a bunch whose beamlets were generated by another code, to which the weight method cannot be applied ([](#shot-scope)).

(shot-counts)=
## Macroparticles and electrons

Four numbers enter the discussion: the number of electrons $N_\lambda$ in a slice, the number of macroparticles $n_p$ in a slice, the number of macroparticles $M$ in a beamlet, and the number of electrons $N$ that a beamlet represents. The first two are defined here and the last two in [](#shot-quiet). The last two must be carefully distinguished.

A slice is one longitudinal sample of the beam. Its length is a whole number of radiation wavelengths, and its macroparticles are distributed over all of those wavelengths ([](fel-physics.md#sec-quiet)). A slice of current $I$ and length $\Delta$ contains

$$
  N_\lambda = \frac{I\,\Delta}{e\,c}
$$ (eq-shot-nlambda)

electrons. The load represents the slice by $n_p$ macroparticles (`beam.n_particle`), each of which carries a weight equal to the charge that it represents.

(shot-quiet)=
## The noise of the macroparticles and the quiet start

Let macroparticle $j$ of a slice represent $\nu_j$ electrons and have the phase $\theta_j$. Its contribution to the radiation source at harmonic $h$ is $\nu_j\,e^{-ih\theta_j}$. With `quiet_start = false` the phases are independent and uniformly distributed over $2\pi$. The cross terms of the squared sum then vanish on average, and

$$
  \Bigl\langle \Bigl| \sum_j \nu_j\, e^{-ih\theta_j} \Bigr|^2 \Bigr\rangle = \sum_j \nu_j^2 = \frac{N_\lambda^2}{n_p} ,
$$ (eq-shot-loud)

where the second equality holds for uniform weights. It is shown in [](#shot-real) that the corresponding quantity for the real beam is $N_\lambda$. The noise power of the macroparticles therefore differs from the shot-noise power by the factor $N_\lambda/n_p$, the number of electrons per macroparticle. This factor has no physical significance, since it changes with `beam.n_particle`. It exceeds 1 wherever a slice contains more electrons than macroparticles, and it is less than 1 in the tails of a bunch.

Both regimes appear in [](#fig-shot-bunch), where the slices have 256 macroparticles. In the peak slice, which contains 1600 electrons, the noise power of the load with `quiet_start = false` is about 6 times the shot-noise power. It is equal to the shot-noise power two rms bunch lengths from the peak, where a slice contains about 256 electrons, and it is $2\times10^{-3}$ of the shot-noise power at four rms bunch lengths. The spectrum of this load is nearly constant above $k_r/3$, between 4.3 and 4.4. This value is the charge-weighted mean of the number of electrons per macroparticle, for which [](#eq-shot-loud) gives 4.42. Below $k_r/3$ the spectrum decreases toward zero, because the charge of each slice of three wavelengths is fixed. Shot noise cannot be added to such a load: with `quiet_start = false` and `shot_noise = true` the loader measures the bunching of the load and refuses a load that already has noise ([](fel-physics.md#sec-noise)).

The quiet start removes this noise. With `quiet_start = true` the macroparticles are generated in beamlets. A beamlet consists of $M$ macroparticles (`beamlet_size`, 8 by default) that have the same transverse coordinates and the same energy. Their phases are evenly spaced over one radiation period,

$$
  \theta_j = \theta_0 + \frac{2\pi j}{M}, \qquad j = 0 \ldots M-1 ,
$$ (eq-shot-quiet)

to each of which a multiple of $2\pi$ is added when the slice is several wavelengths long. This multiple has no effect at any harmonic. The sum of $e^{-ih\theta_j}$ over $j$ vanishes unless $h$ is a multiple of $M$, so that the beamlet has no bunching at any harmonic below $M$. A slice composed of $n_p/M$ such beamlets therefore does not radiate.

The noise of a beamlet is determined by the number of electrons that it represents,

$$
  N = \frac{M\,w}{e} ,
$$ (eq-shot-n)

where $w$ is the weight of one of its macroparticles in the quiet load. For uniform weights, $N = N_\lambda M / n_p$. The quantity $N$ is a number of electrons. It is not the number of macroparticles in the beamlet, which is $M$. At a given current, $N$ increases with the length of the slice (`window.n_wavelength`) and decreases with `beam.n_particle`.

As an example, a slice one wavelength long, at a wavelength of 1 Å and a current of 3 kA, contains $N_\lambda = 6246$ electrons. With 1024 macroparticles in beamlets of 8, the slice has 128 beamlets, each of which represents $N = 48.8$ electrons. A slice in the tail of the same bunch, at $10^{-3}$ of that current, contains 6 electrons, or 0.049 of an electron for each of its 128 beamlets. These two slices are referred to below as the core slice and the tail slice. Without the quiet start the noise power of the core slice would be 6.1 times its shot-noise power, and that of the tail slice 0.006 of its shot-noise power.

(shot-real)=
## The shot noise of N electrons

The bunching of a set of $N$ electrons at harmonic $h$ is

$$
  b_h = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} e^{-i h \theta_j} ,
$$ (eq-shot-b)

where $\theta_j$ is the ponderomotive phase of electron $j$. The electrons of a real beam arrive independently, so that their phases are independent and uniformly distributed over $2\pi$. The cross terms of $|b_h|^2$ then vanish on average, and the $N$ diagonal terms remain:

$$
  \langle |b_h|^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{N} .
$$ (eq-shot-real)

This result holds at every harmonic and at every wavelength between the harmonics. For a slice it becomes $\langle|b_h|^2\rangle = 1/N_\lambda$, and the start-up power of a SASE run is proportional to this quantity.

The quiet start removes the noise of the macroparticles ([](#shot-quiet)), and the loader then imposes the noise of the real beam. Throughout this page a load is characterized by the ratio $R_h(N)$ of the noise power that it imposes to the shot-noise power of the real beam.

(shot-weight)=
## Shot noise imposed through the weights

In this method the phases of the quiet beamlet are left unchanged, and the noise is introduced through the weights of the macroparticles. The method is that of McNeil, Poole and Robb ([](references.md#ref-mcneil)), applied to a beamlet in the manner of Hwang and Qiang ([](references.md#ref-hwang-qiang-weight)).

In the quiet load, each macroparticle of a beamlet of $N$ electrons represents $N/M$ electrons. Suppose instead that macroparticle $j$ represents a whole number $c_j$ of electrons, and that these numbers are drawn as the electrons of a real beam would be distributed: each electron is assigned independently, and with equal probability, to one of the $M$ macroparticles. The numbers $c_j$ are then independent Poisson variables with mean and variance $N/M$. The source term of the beamlet at harmonic $h$ is the sum over its electrons,

$$
  S_h = \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} c_j\, e^{-ih\theta_j} .
$$ (eq-shot-s)

Because the phases are evenly spaced, the mean of $S_h$ vanishes for every $h$ that is not a multiple of $M$. Its mean square is the sum of the variances of the $c_j$:

$$
  \langle |S_h|^2 \rangle = N,
  \qquad \langle |b_h|^2 \rangle = \frac{\langle |S_h|^2 \rangle}{N^2} = \frac{1}{N} .
$$ (eq-shot-weight)

This is the result [](#eq-shot-real) for the real beam, and its derivation involves no approximation. The bunching is linear in the numbers $c_j$, so that no expansion in a small parameter is required. The result holds for any $N$, integral or not, and at every harmonic below $M$, which for the default beamlet are the harmonics 1 to 7. When a beamlet represents about one electron, most of its macroparticles are assigned no electron and a few are assigned one. In that limit the load consists of the individual electrons, each at one of $M$ phases.

### Conservation of the bunch charge

If the numbers $c_j$ were drawn independently, the total charge of the bunch would fluctuate from one load to the next. The loader instead conserves the charge of the bunch. A bunch of charge $Q$ is represented by $n$ electrons, where $n$ is the integer nearest to $Q/e$ and is at least one. The numbers $c_j$ for all the macroparticles of the bunch are then obtained from a single multinomial draw of $n$ electrons, in which macroparticle $j$ has a probability $p_j$ proportional to its weight in the quiet load. The variance of $c_j$ is $n p_j (1 - p_j)$, and the covariance of $c_j$ and $c_k$ is $-n p_j p_k$. Consequently, for any set of macroparticles, whether a slice or a beamlet,

$$
  \langle |S_h|^2 \rangle = n \sum_j p_j \;-\; n\, \Bigl| \sum_j p_j\, e^{-ih\theta_j} \Bigr|^2 .
$$ (eq-shot-multinomial)

The second term is the square of the mean source term, which vanishes for each beamlet separately. The remaining term is the mean number of electrons in the set. The result [](#eq-shot-weight) therefore remains valid when the charge of the bunch is conserved, for a slice of any charge and for any distribution of weight among the beamlets.

Each of the $n$ electrons carries the charge $Q/n$, so that the charge of the load is exactly $Q$. The noise power is proportional to the square of this charge multiplied by $n$, and therefore

$$
  R = \frac{Q/e}{n}
$$ (eq-shot-weight-ratio)

at every harmonic and in every slice. The ratio $R$ differs from 1 by at most $1/(2n)$, which is $10^{-9}$ for the $5\times10^8$ electrons in the window of the FLASH1 example. A bunch of less than half an electron is represented by one electron.

The number of electrons in a slice is a binomial variable with mean $N_\lambda$ and variance $N_\lambda (1 - N_\lambda/n)$. The charge of a slice therefore fluctuates from shot to shot with a relative standard deviation of $1/\sqrt{N_\lambda}$, as it does in a real beam. This fluctuation is 0.7% for the 18740 electrons in a slice of `examples/sase`, and it constitutes the noise of the load at wavelengths longer than the fundamental. On average, the current profile of the load is that of the bunch.

The routine `fel_weight_noise` performs the multinomial draw sequentially. Each slice in turn receives a binomially distributed share of the electrons that remain, and each macroparticle receives a binomially distributed share of the electrons that remain in its slice. The binomial generator `fel_ran_binomial` is exact in distribution. It uses the transformed rejection method of Hörmann ([](references.md#ref-hormann)) when ten or more successes are expected, and otherwise sums the probabilities upward from zero successes. The generator chooses between the two algorithms by comparing the expected number of successes with ten, and it compares the probability with one half. In a load these quantities are often ratios of whole numbers, and they then lie within rounding error of ten or of one half. Both comparisons are therefore made with a small margin, so that the same algorithm is chosen in every build and a given `ran_seed` gives the same shot. The debug and the release build assign the same number of electrons to every macroparticle of the Gaussian bunch of `check_load.py` at eight seeds.

A beamlet in which no macroparticle receives an electron is omitted from the load, so that a slice with few electrons contains approximately one beamlet for each electron. A macroparticle that receives no electron, in a beamlet that is retained, is kept with zero weight, so that the slice still consists of complete beamlets. A slice that receives no electron contains no particles.

### The keep mode

In the keep mode, a beamlet consists of one particle of the bunch and its copies, and every particle retains its own charge. A particle of $N$ electrons is represented by either $k = \lfloor N \rfloor$ or $k + 1$ electrons, each of which carries the charge of the particle divided by that number. The noise power of the particle is proportional to the square of its charge divided by the number of electrons. The number $k$ is chosen with the probability

$$
  P(k) = \frac{k\,(k + 1 - N)}{N} ,
$$ (eq-shot-kept)

for which the mean of the reciprocal of the number of electrons is $1/N$. The ratio $R$ is then 1 for every particle of one electron or more. The electrons are assigned to the $M$ copies with equal probability, so that at least one copy carries charge. A particle of less than one electron is represented by one electron. One of its copies then carries the entire charge of the particle, and the ratio is $R = N$.

(shot-kick)=
## The first-order kick

With `beam.shot_noise_method = "first_order"`, every phase of a beamlet is displaced according to

$$
  \theta_j \;\to\; \theta_j - \sum_{k=1}^{H} a_k \sin(k\theta_j + \phi_k),
  \qquad a_k = \frac{z_k}{k}, \qquad z_k = 2\sqrt{\frac{x_k}{N}} ,
$$ (eq-shot-kick)

where the number of harmonics is $H = (M-1)/2$ in integer division, which is 3 for a beamlet of 8. The sines are evaluated at the unperturbed phases. Two random numbers are drawn for each harmonic of each beamlet. The phase $\phi_k$ is uniformly distributed over $2\pi$, and $x_k = -\ln u_k$, with $u_k$ uniformly distributed on $(0,1)$, is exponentially distributed with unit mean. The kick is intended to give the beamlet a bunching of $\sqrt{x_k/N}$ at harmonic $k$, the square of which has the mean $1/N$ and the exponential distribution of real shot noise. The quantity $z_k$ is the amplitude of the kick in the phase of harmonic $k$. Equation [](#eq-shot-kick) is [](fel-physics.md#eq-fawley) written for one beamlet.

### One harmonic

Consider first a kick at the fundamental alone, and let $z = z_1$. The bunching of the beamlet after the kick is

$$
  b_1 = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j} e^{-i\theta_j}\, e^{\,i z \sin(\theta_j + \phi)}
      = \sum_{n} J_n(z)\, e^{in\phi}\; \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j} e^{\,i(n-1)\theta_j} ,
$$ (eq-shot-ja)

by the Jacobi-Anger expansion. Because the phases are evenly spaced, the sum over $j$ vanishes unless $n - 1$ is a multiple of $M$. The term $n = 1$ remains, and the next terms are of order $z^{M-1}$, which is $1.7\times10^{-4}$ at $z = 2$ for a beamlet of 8. Hence

$$
  |b_1| = J_1(z) .
$$ (eq-shot-j1)

For a small kick, $J_1(z) \approx z/2 = \sqrt{x/N}$, which is the intended bunching. The algorithm is designed in this way: the amplitude of the kick is chosen so that the bunching to first order in the kick has the shot-noise mean. The kick is small, however, only when $N$ is large. Its typical amplitude is $2/\sqrt{N}$, which is 0.16 rad at 150 electrons, 0.47 rad at 18 and 2 rad at one. As $z$ increases, $J_1(z)$ falls below $z/2$, and it reaches its maximum of 0.58 at $z = 1.84$.

:::{figure} generated/shot-noise/kick.svg
:name: fig-shot-kick
:alt: The Bessel function J1 of z and the straight line z over 2 as functions of the kick amplitude z from 0 to 4 radians, with three shaded distributions of the kick amplitude for 150, 18 and 1 electrons per beamlet. The two curves separate near z of 0.5.
The bunching $|b_1|$ produced in a beamlet by a kick of amplitude $z$, which is $J_1(z)$, compared with the first-order value $z/2$. The shaded curves are the distributions of the kick amplitudes drawn at 150, 18 and 1 electrons per beamlet, each scaled to the same height.
:::

The average over the distribution of $x$ is Weber's second exponential integral ([](references.md#ref-watson)),

$$
  \langle |b_1|^2 \rangle = \int_0^\infty e^{-x}\, J_1^2\!\bigl(2\sqrt{x/N}\bigr)\, dx
     = e^{-2/N}\, I_1(2/N) ,
  \qquad R(N) = N\, e^{-2/N}\, I_1(2/N) = 1 - \frac{2}{N} + \frac{5}{2N^2} - \ldots
$$ (eq-shot-r1)

where $I_1$ is the modified Bessel function. The same result holds for a kick at any single harmonic.

### The harmonics together

The kick of [](#eq-shot-kick) is a sum over $H$ harmonics, and the kick at each harmonic also changes the bunching at the others. The mean square bunching is obtained from a sum over pairs of macroparticles:

$$
  |b_h|^2 = \frac{1}{M^2} \sum_{j,l} e^{-ih(\theta_j - \theta_l)}
     \prod_{k=1}^{H} \exp\!\Bigl( i h a_k \bigl[ \sin(k\theta_j + \phi_k) - \sin(k\theta_l + \phi_k) \bigr] \Bigr) .
$$ (eq-shot-pairs)

The difference of the two sines is $2 \sin(k\delta/2) \cos(\psi_k)$, where $\delta = \theta_j - \theta_l$ and $\psi_k$ is uniformly distributed. The average of $e^{i\zeta\cos\psi}$ over a uniformly distributed $\psi$ is $J_0(\zeta)$, so that the average over $\phi_k$ of the factor for harmonic $k$ is $J_0(2\sqrt{\beta_k x_k})$, with $\beta_k = (2h^2/k^2 N)(1 - \cos k\delta)$. The average over the exponential distribution of $x_k$ follows from term-by-term integration of the series for $J_0$, which gives Weber's first exponential integral:

$$
  \int_0^\infty e^{-x} J_0\!\bigl(2\sqrt{\beta x}\bigr)\, dx = e^{-\beta} .
$$ (eq-shot-weber0)

The random numbers for different harmonics are independent, and the pair $(j, l)$ enters only through $\delta = 2\pi(j-l)/M$. Therefore

$$
  \langle |b_h|^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \cos\frac{2\pi j h}{M}\;
    \exp\!\Bigl( -\frac{2h^2}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{H} \frac{1 - \cos(2\pi j k/M)}{k^2} \Bigr) ,
  \qquad R_h(N) = N\, \langle |b_h|^2 \rangle .
$$ (eq-shot-rh)

This expression is exact for the kick of [](#eq-shot-kick). It is a sum of $M$ terms, and it is evaluated by `fel_shot_ratio` and by `tests/scripts/shot_noise_ratio.py`. For a single harmonic and in the limit of large $M$ it reduces to [](#eq-shot-r1). A harmonic above $H$ receives no kick, and its mean square bunching is of order $1/N^2$. To second order in the kick, the default beamlet has

$$
  R_1 \approx 1 - \frac{22}{9N}, \qquad R_2 \approx 1 - \frac{1.01}{N}, \qquad R_3 \approx 1 - \frac{2.00}{N} .
$$ (eq-shot-second)

The deficit at the fundamental exceeds the $2/N$ of [](#eq-shot-r1), because the kicks at the second and third harmonics also spread the phases of the fundamental.

For a slice whose beamlets represent different numbers of electrons, the ratio is the charge-weighted mean of the ratios of the beamlets,

$$
  \langle |b_h|^2 \rangle\, N_\lambda = \frac{\sum_g N_g\, R_h(N_g)}{\sum_g N_g} ,
$$ (eq-shot-slice)

where $N_g$ is the number of electrons represented by beamlet $g$, because the kicks of different beamlets are independent.

(shot-clamp)=
### Below one electron

For $N < 1$ the kick would be larger than the kick at one electron. The loader therefore replaces $N$ by 1 in the kick whenever $N$ is less than 1. The bunching of the beamlet is then that of a beamlet of one electron, whatever its charge, and

$$
  R_h(N) = N\, R_h(1), \qquad N < 1 .
$$ (eq-shot-clamp)

For the default beamlet, $R_1(1) = 0.161$. The tail slice of [](#shot-counts), with 6 electrons in 128 beamlets, would start with 0.008 of its shot-noise power, that is, with $\langle|b|^2\rangle = 0.0013$ where the real slice has $1/6$.

A beamlet of less than one electron occurs in three cases: in a slice that contains less than one electron in total, which has a single beamlet according to [](#eq-shot-fewer), in the keep mode, and in the sample mode with `beam.beamlet_min_electrons = 0`. The report of the load gives the number of slices of the first kind. For the slices that contain one electron or more and have a group of less than one electron, it issues the following warning, with the level computed from [](#eq-shot-slice):

```
<count> slices have a noise group of less than one real electron.
They hold <share> of the charge that carries noise, and the noise power loaded
in them is <ratio> of their shot-noise power at the fundamental.
```

The loader also reduces a kick $a_k$ larger than $2\pi$ modulo $2\pi$. At one electron this affects a fraction $e^{-\pi^2} = 5\times10^{-5}$ of the kicks at the fundamental, and the closed form neglects it.

(shot-fewer)=
### Fewer beamlets in a slice of little charge

With the first-order kick and the quiet start, the sample mode loads a slice of $N_\lambda$ electrons with

$$
  n_b = \min\!\left(\frac{n_p}{M},\ \max\!\left(1,\ \left\lfloor \frac{N_\lambda}{N_{\min}} \right\rfloor\right)\right)
$$ (eq-shot-fewer)

beamlets, where $N_{\min}$ is `beam.beamlet_min_electrons`, which is 1 by default (`fel_beamlet_count`). The charge of the slice is unchanged: the weight of each macroparticle is the charge of the slice divided by $n_b M$. A slice of $N_{\min}$ electrons or more then has no beamlet of fewer than $N_{\min}$ electrons, and a slice of fewer than $N_{\min}$ electrons has a single beamlet. The generator draws $n_b$ base samples for the slice, and the [resampler](glossary.md#gl-resampler) reduces the candidates of the slice to $n_b$ seeds by the same random deletion that otherwise reduces them to $n_p/M$. The rule does not apply to the keep mode, which retains every particle of the bunch, or to the weight load, which does not read the key. In the sample mode a slice with no charge contains no particles under either method, unless the key is 0.

With the default value, the tail slice of [](#shot-counts) has 6 beamlets of one electron each and starts with 0.161 of its shot-noise power, compared with 0.008 for 128 beamlets. A larger $N_{\min}$ raises the level in the slices that it affects, to 0.56 at 4 electrons, 0.87 at 18 and 0.95 at 50, and it affects more slices. With $N_{\min} = 18$ the number of beamlets in the core slice is reduced when the slice has 2784 macroparticles or more, and with $N_{\min} = 50$ when it has 1000 or more. The FLASH1 example, a Gaussian bunch of 351 slices with 512 beamlets per slice, was loaded and tracked with six seeds at each of five values on 2026-11-10:

| $N_{\min}$ | slices with fewer beamlets | their share of the charge | macroparticles | noise power of the load over shot-noise power, window | exit pulse energy, six seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 1437696 | 0.9995 | 48.2 ± 2.7 µJ |
| 1 | 146 | 1.0e-5 | 880832 | 0.9995 | 49.9 ± 1.9 µJ |
| 4 | 162 | 4.7e-5 | 821904 | 0.9995 | 48.8 ± 2.0 µJ |
| 18 | 180 | 2.3e-4 | 752544 | 0.9995 | 48.2 ± 1.5 µJ |
| 50 | 192 | 6.2e-4 | 701472 | 0.9996 | 45.4 ± 1.5 µJ |

The differences among the five values are smaller than the shot-to-shot spread. In a separate measurement with twelve seeds, a Gaussian bunch in one undulator starts with 0.99 ± 0.06 of the power of the flat window at $N_{\min} = 0$ and with 0.98 ± 0.04 at $N_{\min} = 1$ ([](validation.md#val-shot-noise-under-weights)). The measurements therefore do not distinguish among the values. The default, $N_{\min} = 1$, is the value at which a beamlet is removed from a slice only if the slice has no electron for the beamlet to represent.

A slice with fewer beamlets has correspondingly fewer samples of the transverse distribution and of the energy spread. The `Load` line of the header gives the number of beamlets in a slice that was not reduced and the smallest number in any slice that contains particles. The [device](glossary.md#gl-device) and the [FP32](glossary.md#gl-fp32) [twin](glossary.md#gl-twin) use the number of particles in each slice, and the residual guard of the twin is evaluated on the slices of 256 particles or more ([](performance.md)). For a given `ran_seed`, a load in which some slice is reduced is a different shot from the load with the key set to 0, and a load in which no slice is reduced is identical to it bit for bit. Setting `beam.beamlet_min_electrons = 0` gives every slice $n_p/M$ beamlets, as in Genesis4, and the [tiers](glossary.md#gl-tier) that compare a load with that of Genesis4 use this setting.

(shot-measured)=
## Comparison of the load with the closed forms

The script `check_shot_noise.py` loads a uniform bunch of 256 slices with 15 seeds and computes the bunching of every slice from the particle dump. It is run in every pass of the [validation suite](glossary.md#gl-validation-suite), and its results are tabulated in [](validation.md#val-levels-shot-noise). It makes three groups of comparisons.

- The weight load is tested at 146, 18, 4, 1 and 0.25 electrons per beamlet. At each of harmonics 1 to 7 the measured ratio must agree with [](#eq-shot-weight-ratio) to within five standard deviations of the mean. The charge of the load must equal that of the input file to $10^{-12}$, every weight must be an integral multiple of the charge of one electron of the load, and the variance of the number of electrons in a slice must agree with the binomial variance. A slice that receives no electron must contain no particles, and in the keep mode every particle of the bunch must be present with its own charge.
- The first-order kick is tested at 146, 50, 18, 4 and 0.25 electrons per beamlet, with uniform weights and with beamlet weights that alternate between 0.25 and 1.75 of the mean. At harmonics 1 to 3 the measured ratio must agree with [](#eq-shot-slice), and the level printed by the loader must agree with it to the four decimal places printed. A Gaussian bunch must have the charge and the number of beamlets given by [](#eq-shot-fewer). The keep mode, and two loads whose beamlets were generated outside the loader, are compared with the same expression.
- The distribution of the macroparticles over a slice is tested for slices of 3 and of 12 wavelengths ([](fel-physics.md#sec-quiet)). Without noise, the sum of $w\,e^{-i(m/n)\theta}$ over a slice, for every $m$ that is not a multiple of the number $n$ of wavelengths in the slice, must be less than $10^{-12}$ of the charge of the slice. With the weight load, the square of this sum must agree with the shot noise. With the first-order kick the value is recorded and is not tested: it is 0.36 of the shot noise at 3 wavelengths and 0.46 at 12.

The points in [](#fig-shot-ratio) and [](#fig-shot-weight) are these measured values. The data for [](#fig-shot-bunch) are written by `tests/scripts/shot_noise_spectrum.py`, which does not write a record in which the spectrum of the weight load differs from 1 by more than 0.05 in any band.

(shot-scope)=
## Scope

The weight load requires the beamlets generated by this loader, in either load mode. With `"weight"`, a load without the quiet start is refused, and the message names `"first_order"` as the alternative.

The closed form [](#eq-shot-rh) applies to a beamlet of evenly spaced phases that is kicked by `fel_fawley_noise`. This includes the beamlets generated by the loader in both load modes, and the evenly spaced copies in a bunch file written by the loader of another code, to which `fel_group_noise` applies the same kick. When the groups are the occupants of a deposit cell, the limit of [](#eq-shot-rh) for large $M$ applies, with $N$ the number of electrons in the whole group. A group whose phases are not evenly spaced copies is not treated here.

This page concerns the load alone. The amplification of the noise by the FEL, and the dependence of the start-up power on the transverse grid, are treated in [](startup-noise.md).
